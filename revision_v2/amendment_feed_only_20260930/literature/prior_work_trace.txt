1. CONFIRMED: active input design for fault diagnosis is established prior work.
Scott, Findeisen, Braatz and Raimondo, "Input design for guaranteed fault diagnosis using zonotopes," Automatica 50 (2014), 1580-1589; doi:10.1016/j.automatica.2014.03.016. Section 2 (PDF p2 / journal p1581) specifies linear fault scenarios and zonotopically bounded initial states, disturbances and measurement errors. Section 4, Definition 1 and Theorem 3 (PDF p3 / p1582), characterizes separation through disjoint reachable output sets. Section 5 (PDF p4 / p1583), especially (18)-(33), optimizes a quadratic input cost subject to separation and input constraints, via a mixed-integer quadratic program. Section 9 (PDF pp9-10 / pp1588-1589) explicitly conditions guaranteed diagnosis on existence of separating inputs. Do not attribute invention of all active diagnosis to this paper: its introduction itself reviews earlier work.

2. CONFIRMED, with assumptions: closed-loop diagnostic input optimization also predates this study.
Raimondo, Marseglia, Braatz and Scott, "Closed-loop input design for guaranteed fault diagnosis using set-valued observers," Automatica 74 (2016), 107-117; doi:10.1016/j.automatica.2016.07.033. Section 1.1 (PDF p2 / p108) assumes one member of a linear model family remains active during the test and uncertainties lie within bounded convex polytopes. Sections 3-4 (PDF pp3-5 / pp109-111) use constrained zonotopes and measurement-updated observers in moving-horizon input design. Algorithm 1 and Theorem 5 (PDF p5 / p111) give finite-horizon diagnosis conditional on a finite feasible initial separation horizon. Section 5 (PDF pp6-7 / pp112-113) adds an explicit offline-computed alternative with observability assumptions. These are conditional model-based guarantees, not unconditional guarantees for arbitrary nonlinear deterioration.

3. CONFIRMED novelty guard: membrane-reformer control and the hydrogen/feed trade-off already exist.
Serra, Ocampo-Martinez, Li and Llorca, "Model Predictive Control for Ethanol Steam Reformers with Membrane Separation," supplied Postprint_Serra2017.pdf. Section 3.8 (PDF/printed pp11-12) derives a reduced linear prediction model; Sections 4.1.3-4.3 (pp14-15) formulate hydrogen-flow tracking plus ethanol-inlet minimization and receding-horizon control. Section 5 (pp16-17) reports simulations; Section 6 (p17) reiterates these contributions. The abstract distinguishes experimental model fitting from controller simulation. This supports prior membrane ESR control, not a claim that the supplied study's aging diagnosis has already been solved or experimentally validated.
